# Bibliography of Indus Script Research (Verified Edition)

## Notes on Use

- **Date of verification**: 2026-11-25
- Each entry was checked against online records (publisher pages, DOI records, arXiv, the ACL Anthology, PubMed Central, library catalogues, authors' official publication lists, or reliable review and citation pages).
- **Verified at**: the page on which the bibliographic details were confirmed. This does not mean that the full text was consulted; in most cases the check was made at the level of the bibliographic record and the abstract.
- Details that could not be confirmed (page counts, series numbers, DOIs, etc.) have been left out. Where sources give different values, this is stated in the note.
- Entries whose existence or core bibliographic details could not be confirmed are collected separately in the "Unverified" section at the end.
- Citations are given in their original language; descriptions are in English.

## A. Primary sources: corpora, concordances, photographic volumes

**A1.** Mahadevan, Iravatham. 1977. *The Indus Script: Texts, Concordance and Tables*. Memoirs of the Archaeological Survey of India 77. New Delhi: Archaeological Survey of India. viii + 829 pp., 7 pls.
- Verified at: https://zenon.dainst.org/Record/000009025 ; https://cir.nii.ac.jp/crid/1130000794712732544?lang=en
- Note: The standard corpus, containing a list of 417 signs together with texts, a concordance and tables. Several later computational studies (Rao et al. 2009, Yadav et al. 2010, Mukhopadhyay 2019) used a digital version of this corpus.

**A2.** Indus Research Centre, Roja Muthiah Research Library (Chennai). Online edition of the Mahadevan 1977 concordance.
- Verified at: https://www.harappa.com/blog/indus-concordance-and-tamil-potsherds-now-online
- Note: An online resource that makes the out-of-print A1 freely available. The same institution is also reported to have released a searchable collection of more than 15,000 items of pottery graffiti from Tamil Nadu. The release date could not be confirmed.

**A3.** Koskenniemi, Kimmo & Asko Parpola. 1979. *Corpus of Texts in the Indus Script*. Department of Asian and African Studies, University of Helsinki, Research Reports 1. Helsinki. 179 pp.
- Verified at: https://data.nationallibrary.fi/bib/me/I00151096800 ; Parpola's publication list (https://www.ae-info.org/ae/User/Parpola_Asko/Publications, confirmed from a search-result summary)
- Note: The computerized corpus of the Helsinki team. A lecture presentation (IMSc) states that the sign list from the 1979–82 work has 394 signs and was not updated afterwards.

**A4.** Koskenniemi, Kimmo & Asko Parpola. 1982. *A Concordance to the Texts in the Indus Script*. Department of Asian and African Studies, University of Helsinki, Research Reports. Helsinki.
- Verified at: https://www.ae-info.org/ae/User/Parpola_Asko/Publications?skin=raw (confirmed from a search-result summary)
- Note: A concordance to A3. The series number is not given because search results disagree between 2 and 3. This is a different book from the 1973 *Materials for the Study of the Indus Script I* (Seppo Koskenniemi, Asko Parpola, Simo Parpola).

**A5.** Joshi, Jagat Pati & Asko Parpola (eds.), with Erja Lahdenperä & Virpi Hämeen-Anttila. 1987. *Corpus of Indus Seals and Inscriptions*, Vol. 1: *Collections in India*. Helsinki: Suomalainen Tiedeakatemia. ISBN 951-41-0555-9.
- Verified at: https://keibi.uni-tuebingen.de/Record/KEI00040937 ; https://catalog.hathitrust.org/Record/002518146
- Note: The first volume of the CISI photographic series, with photographs of seals and inscriptions held in India. It is the reference source for checking sign readings against photographs of the originals.

**A6.** Shah, Sayid Ghulam Mustafa & Asko Parpola (eds.), in collaboration with Ahmad Nabi Khan et al. 1991. *Corpus of Indus Seals and Inscriptions*, Vol. 2: *Collections in Pakistan*. Helsinki: Suomalainen Tiedeakatemia.
- Verified at: https://libcat.library.tamu.edu/Record/in00001386121/Description ; https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/antiquity/article/book-reviews-asko-parpola-bm-pande-petteri-koskikallio-ed-with-richard-h-meadow-j-mark-kenoyer-corpus-of-indus-seals-and-inscriptions-volume-3-new-material-untraced-objects-and-collections-outside-india-and-pakistan-part-1-mohenjodaro-and-harappa-annales-academiae-scientiarum-fennicae-humaniora-359memoirs-of-the-archaeological-survey-of-india-96-lx443-pages-9-figures-in-text-2-tables-363-pages-of-bw-plates-48-pages-of-colour-plates-2010-helsinki-suomaleinen-tiedeakatemia-9789514110405-hardback-295/505D12B99A81BB72F4396A256CCEA26B
- Note: Covers collections held in Pakistan (including many finds from Mohenjo-daro and Harappa). The year 1991 was confirmed from the series record (1987–1991) and from the description in a review of Vol. 3.1.

**A7.** Parpola, Asko, B. M. Pande & Petteri Koskikallio (eds.), in collaboration with Richard H. Meadow & J. Mark Kenoyer. 2010. *Corpus of Indus Seals and Inscriptions*, Vol. 3: *New Material, Untraced Objects, and Collections Outside India and Pakistan*, Part 1: *Mohenjo-daro and Harappa*. Annales Academiae Scientiarum Fennicae, Humaniora 359; Memoirs of the Archaeological Survey of India 96. Helsinki: Suomalainen Tiedeakatemia. lx + 443 pp. ISBN 978-951-41-1040-5.
- Verified at: https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/antiquity/article/book-reviews-asko-parpola-bm-pande-petteri-koskikallio-ed-with-richard-h-meadow-j-mark-kenoyer-corpus-of-indus-seals-and-inscriptions-volume-3-new-material-untraced-objects-and-collections-outside-india-and-pakistan-part-1-mohenjodaro-and-harappa-annales-academiae-scientiarum-fennicae-humaniora-359memoirs-of-the-archaeological-survey-of-india-96-lx443-pages-9-figures-in-text-2-tables-363-pages-of-bw-plates-48-pages-of-colour-plates-2010-helsinki-suomaleinen-tiedeakatemia-9789514110405-hardback-295/505D12B99A81BB72F4396A256CCEA26B ; https://ci.nii.ac.jp/ncid/BB03724813
- Note: Supplements the earlier volumes with new material and untraced objects from Mohenjo-daro and Harappa. One review gives the page count as 444.

**A8.** Parpola, Asko, B. M. Pande & Petteri Koskikallio (eds.). 2019. *Corpus of Indus Seals and Inscriptions*, Vol. 3.2: *New Material, Untraced Objects, and Collections Outside India and Pakistan*, Part 2. Annales Academiae Scientiarum Fennicae, Humaniora 383. Helsinki: Suomalainen Tiedeakatemia. xxxvii + 394 pp.
- Verified at: https://www.antiquity.ac.uk/node/4305 ; https://tiedekirja.fi/sv/corpus-of-indus-seals-and-inscriptions-3-2
- Note: Includes material from many sites, among them Shahr-i Sokhta, Mundigak, Mehrgarh and Nausharo, Chanhudaro, Kalibangan and Rojdi.

**A9.** Parpola, Asko & Petteri Koskikallio (eds.). 2022. *Corpus of Indus Seals and Inscriptions*, Vol. 3.3: *Indo-Iranian Borderlands*. Helsinki: Suomalainen Tiedeakatemia.
- Verified at: https://the-past.com/review/books/corpus-of-indus-seals-and-inscriptions-volume-3-3/ ; https://orbi.uliege.be/bitstream/2268/333796/1/Desset%20F%202022%20in%20CISI%203.3%201763.pdf
- Note: Covers material from surrounding regions, including the western highlands of Pakistan, Afghanistan, Central Asia and China. According to a review, a final volume, 3.4, is to follow.

**A10.** Wells, Bryan K. 2011. *Epigraphic Approaches to Indus Writing*. American School of Prehistoric Research Monograph. Oxford: Oxbow Books. ISBN 978-1-84217-994-9.
- Verified at: https://www.oxbowbooks.com/9781842179949/epigraphic-approaches-to-indus-writing/ ; https://zenon.dainst.org/Record/000858634/Description
- Note: Deals with a computerized corpus, a detailed sign list, numeral signs and the problem of defining signs. The page count differs between sources: 272 pp. according to the publisher, 243 pp. according to the DAI catalogue.

**A11.** Wells, Bryan K. 2015. *The Archaeology and Epigraphy of Indus Writing*. With technical appendices by Andreas Fuls. Oxford: Archaeopress. ISBN 978-1-78491-046-4 (pbk); 978-1-78491-047-1 (digital).
- Verified at: https://www.archaeopress.com/Archaeopress/Products/9781784910464 ; https://eld.bl.uk/catalog/019967455
- Note: Fuls's appendices deal with automatic segmentation, analysis of sign positions and the classification of undeciphered scripts. Daggumati & Revesz (2021) cite the sign count of this book as 694.

**A12.** Wells, Bryan K. & Andreas Fuls. *Interactive Corpus of Indus Texts* (ICIT). Online database, TU Berlin (in development since 2008).
- Verified at: https://www.user.tu-berlin.de/fuls/Homepage/indus/menueindus.htm
- Note: An online database of inscriptions developed jointly by Wells and Fuls; access is granted on request to the administrators. It is described as using a sign system of more than 700 signs. A recent computational study (Nair 2026, D16) used a digitized version of this resource.

**A13.** Fuls, Andreas. 2022. *Corpus of Indus Inscriptions*. Mathematica Epigraphica 3. Berlin: independently published. (2nd ed. 2023.)
- Verified at: https://www.harappa.com/content/corpus-indus-inscriptions ; https://harappa.com/node/5462
- Note: A printed corpus of inscriptions based on the ICIT work.

**A14.** Fuls, Andreas. 2023. *Catalog of Indus Signs*. Mathematica Epigraphica 4. Berlin: independently published. ISBN 979-8-398-42230-6.
- Verified at: https://www.harappa.com/content/catalog-indus-signs ; https://www.academia.edu/103538728
- Note: A sign list giving, for each sign, a description, its frequency and its positional behaviour. The total page count could not be confirmed.

## B. Decipherment attempts and synthetic studies

**B1.** Parpola, Asko. 1994. *Deciphering the Indus Script*. Cambridge: Cambridge University Press. xxii + 374 pp. ISBN 0-521-43079-8 (hb). Paperback reissue 2009, ISBN 978-0-521-79566-1.
- Verified at: https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/antiquity/article/abs/on-not-breaking-the-indus-code/6CBA666292D6ADEBA418B573BBAF1014
- Note: The leading synthesis of the view that the Indus script is a logo-syllabic script recording a Dravidian language. The review cited here was written by M. D. Coe.

**B2.** Zide, Arlene R. K. & Kamil V. Zvelebil (eds.). 1976. *The Soviet Decipherment of the Indus Valley Script: Translation and Critique*. Janua Linguarum, Series Practica 156. The Hague: Mouton. 142 pp. (Reprint De Gruyter Mouton, DOI 10.1515/9783110800258.)
- Verified at: https://libcat.library.tamu.edu/Record/in00000097613/Details ; https://biblioteca.izt.uam.mx/vufind/Record/EBSCO_ocn558101505/TOC
- Note: Contains English translations of, and critiques of, the Knorozov team's 1965 *Preliminary Report on the Investigation of the Proto-Indian Texts* and 1968 *Proto-Indica*. It is the main way to read the work of the Soviet school in English.

**B3.** Knorozov, Yu. V., M. F. Albedil & B. Ya. Volchok. 1981. *Proto-Indica: 1979. Report on the Investigation of the Proto-Indian Texts*. Moscow: Nauka.
- Verified at: https://eo.iea.ras.ru/wp-content/uploads/1984/05/eoarchive_1984_5_148_dubjanskij.pdf ; https://eo.iea.ras.ru/wp-content/uploads/1983/01/eoarchive_1983_1_151_knorozov_spisok-rabot.pdf
- Note: A 1984 review describes this book as a comprehensive report summarizing the results of the research up to that time.

**B4.** Fairservis, Walter A. 1992. *The Harappan Civilization and its Writing: A Model for the Decipherment of the Indus Script*. Leiden & New York: E. J. Brill. viii + 239 pp. ISBN 90-04-09066-5. (Indian ed.: New Delhi: Oxford & IBH, ISBN 81-204-0491-2.)
- Verified at: https://libcat.library.tamu.edu/Record/in00001068020 ; https://list.indology.info/pipermail/indology/1994-November/001613.html
- Note: A decipherment model that starts from the archaeological context and proposes Dravidian readings. It is one of the attempts reviewed by Possehl (1996).

**B5.** Possehl, Gregory L. 1996. *Indus Age: The Writing System*. Philadelphia: University of Pennsylvania Press. xvi + 244 pp.
- Verified at: https://www.sas.upenn.edu/~gpossehl/IndusAge.html ; https://cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-asian-studies/article/abs/indus-age-the-writing-system-by-gregory-l-possehl-philadelphia-university-of-pennsylvania-press-1996-xvi-244-pp-4500-cloth/BC34785D6BD87FA15E93C32F39B7DF19
- Note: A history of research reviewing more than 40 decipherment attempts, from Hunter in 1929 to Fairservis in 1992. The author concludes that no independently verified decipherment yet exists.

**B6.** Mahadevan, Iravatham. 2009. "Vestiges of Indus Civilization in Old Tamil." Tamilnadu Endowment Lecture, Tamilnadu History Congress, 16th Annual Session, Tiruchirapalli, 9–11 October 2009. PDF: Roja Muthiah Research Library.
- Verified at: https://rmrl.in/wp-content/plugins/book_lists/admin/pdf_books/36%20-%20Vestiges%20of%20Indus%20Civilization%20in%20Old%20Tamil%20(2009).pdf
- Note: One of Mahadevan's later works, which tries to link Indus signs with titles and customs found in Old Tamil literature.

**B7.** Mahadevan, Iravatham. 2014. *Dravidian Proof of the Indus Script via the Rig Veda: A Case Study*. Bulletin of the Indus Research Centre No. 4 (Gift Siromoney Endowment Lecture 2014). Chennai: Roja Muthiah Research Library.
- Verified at: https://rmrl.in/bulletin/bulletin-No-4-Nov-2014.pdf ; https://www.harappa.com/content/dravidian-proof-indus-script-rig-veda-case-study
- Note: Matches logographic signs with roots in the Dravidian Etymological Dictionary (DEDR). A 2023 review judges the Dravidian hypothesis to be possible but not proven (https://journal.fi/store/article/view/85246/78240).

**B8.** Mukhopadhyay, Bahata Ansumali. 2019. "Interrogating Indus inscriptions to unravel their mechanisms of meaning conveyance." *Palgrave Communications* 5(1): 1–37. DOI: 10.1057/s41599-019-0274-1.
- Verified at: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41599-019-0274-1 ; https://ideas.repec.org/a/pal/palcom/v5y2019i1d10.1057_s41599-019-0274-1.html
- Note: Analyses how meaning is conveyed through patterns of sign combination, without assigning sound values, and argues that the signs were mainly logographic. A digital version of the Mahadevan corpus was used. (Volume and pages follow the RePEc record.)

**B9.** Mukhopadhyay, Bahata Ansumali. 2023. "Semantic scope of Indus inscriptions comprising taxation, trade and craft licensing, commodity control and access control: archaeological and script-internal evidence." *Humanities and Social Sciences Communications* 10: 972. DOI: 10.1057/s41599-023-02320-7.
- Verified at: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41599-023-02320-7 ; https://doaj.org/article/76639d3282f9492a900a4ae9545ea93a
- Note: Extends B8 by treating seals and tablets as media carrying administrative information and examining them together with their archaeological distribution. Three corpora were used (including an online interactive corpus and the Mahadevan corpus).

**B10.** Yajnadevam (Bharath Rao). n.d. "A cryptanalytic decipherment of the Indus Script." Preprint, Academia.edu (not peer-reviewed).
- Verified at: https://academia.edu/78867798/A_cryptanalytic_decipherment_of_the_Indus_Script ; https://list.indology.info/pipermail/indology/2025-March/060315.html ; https://theprint.in/feature/here-are-two-indians-whove-tried-to-crack-the-indus-script-in-last-10-years/2452190/
- Note: Claims to read Indus inscriptions as Sanskrit using cryptanalytic techniques. The claim has not been accepted by scholars. On the Indology mailing list it was criticized for, among other things, the lack of peer review and the ease of forcing one-to-one matches when a large dictionary is available. The year of posting could not be confirmed.

## C. The debate over whether the signs are writing

**C1.** Farmer, Steve, Richard Sproat & Michael Witzel. 2004. "The collapse of the Indus-script thesis: The myth of a literate Harappan civilization." *Electronic Journal of Vedic Studies* 11(2): 19–57.
- Verified at: https://hasp.ub.uni-heidelberg.de/journals/ejvs/article/view/620
- Note: Argues that the Indus signs are not a script recording language, citing among other things the shortness of the inscriptions and the large number of signs that occur only once. It became the starting point of the later debate.

**C2.** Parpola, Asko. 2008. "Is the Indus script indeed not a writing system?" In *Airavati: Felicitation Volume in Honour of Iravatham Mahadevan*, 111–131. Chennai: Varalaaru.com.
- Verified at: https://www.harappa.com/content/indus-script-indeed-not-writing-system ; https://list.indology.info/pipermail/indology/2025-March/060322.html
- Note: A rebuttal of C1 that defends the view of the Indus script as a logo-syllabic script of the Sumerian type.

**C3.** Rao, Rajesh P. N., Nisha Yadav, Mayank N. Vahia, Hrishikesh Joglekar, R. Adhikari & Iravatham Mahadevan. 2009. "Entropic evidence for linguistic structure in the Indus script." *Science* 324: 1165. DOI: 10.1126/science.1170391.
- Verified at: https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2009Sci...324.1165R/abstract ; https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~rao/ScienceIndus.pdf
- Note: Reports that the conditional entropy of the Indus script is closer to that of natural languages than to that of non-linguistic systems. The authors state that this adds to the evidence rather than proving linguistic status.

**C4.** Sproat, Richard. 2010. "Last Words: Ancient symbols, computational linguistics, and the reviewing practices of the general science journals." *Computational Linguistics* 36(3): 585–594. DOI: 10.1162/coli_a_00011.
- Verified at: https://aclanthology.org/J10-3012/
- Note: Criticizes the methodology of C3 and of Lee et al. (C6), as well as the reviewing practices of general science journals.

**C5.** Rao, Rajesh P. N., Nisha Yadav, Mayank N. Vahia, Hrishikesh Joglekar, R. Adhikari & Iravatham Mahadevan. 2010. "Commentary and Discussion: Entropy, the Indus script, and language: A reply to R. Sproat." *Computational Linguistics* 36(4): 795–805. DOI: 10.1162/coli_c_00030.
- Verified at: https://aclanthology.org/J10-4016/
- Note: A reply to C4, arguing that the critic attacked a straw man, namely the claim that similar entropy alone shows that a system is language.

**C6.** Lee, Rob, Philip Jonathan & Pauline Ziman. 2010. "Pictish symbols revealed as a written language through application of Shannon entropy." *Proceedings of the Royal Society A* 466(2121): 2545–2560. DOI: 10.1098/rspa.2010.0041.
- Verified at: https://eprints.lancs.ac.uk/id/eprint/133087 ; https://www.lancaster.ac.uk/~jonathan/PRSA10.pdf
- Note: Classifies Pictish symbols as writing using a bigram entropy measure adjusted for small samples. It is a comparative case in the same methodological debate as the Indus research.

**C7.** Sproat, Richard. 2014. "A statistical comparison of written language and nonlinguistic symbol systems." *Language* 90(2): 457–481.
- Verified at: https://muse.jhu.edu/article/547119/figure/fig02 ; https://research.google/pubs/a-statistical-comparison-of-written-language-and-nonlinguistic-symbol-systems/
- Note: Tests the question on larger non-linguistic and linguistic corpora and reports that the statistical measures proposed earlier are not useful in their published form, and that the Indus and Pictish systems are classified on the non-linguistic side. The non-linguistic comparison corpora were used again in D16.

## D. Computational and statistical methods

**D1.** Rao, Rajesh P. N., Nisha Yadav, Mayank N. Vahia, Hrishikesh Joglekar, R. Adhikari & Iravatham Mahadevan. 2009. "A Markov model of the Indus script." *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences* 106(33): 13685–13690. DOI: 10.1073/pnas.0906237106.
- Verified at: https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1073%2FPNAS.0906237106 ; https://dblp.uni-trier.de/rec/journals/pnas/RaoYVJAM09.html
- Note: Captures dependencies in sign order with a first-order Markov (bigram) model and applies it to restoring missing signs in damaged inscriptions.

**D2.** Yadav, Nisha, Hrishikesh Joglekar, Rajesh P. N. Rao, Mayank N. Vahia, Ronojoy Adhikari & Iravatham Mahadevan. 2010. "Statistical analysis of the Indus script using n-grams." *PLoS ONE* 5(3): e9506. DOI: 10.1371/journal.pone.0009506. arXiv: 0901.3017.
- Verified at: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2841631 ; https://www.arxiv.org/abs/0901.3017
- Note: Extracts significant sign pairs and triplets with n-gram models and reports an accuracy of about 75% in restoring doubtful readings. It does not conclude that the script records a natural language.

**D3.** Rao, Rajesh P. N. 2010. "Probabilistic analysis of an ancient undeciphered script." *Computer* (IEEE) 43(4): 76–80. DOI: 10.1109/MC.2010.112.
- Verified at: https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~rao/ieeeIndus.pdf ; https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~rao/indus.html
- Note: An invited paper introducing the Markov-model approach of D1 and D2 to a general readership.

**D4.** Sinha, Sitabhra, Md Izhar Ashraf, Raj Kumar Pan & Bryan Kenneth Wells. 2011. "Network analysis of a corpus of undeciphered Indus civilization inscriptions indicates syntactic organization." *Computer Speech and Language* 25: 639–654. DOI: 10.1016/j.csl.2010.05.007. arXiv: 1005.4997.
- Verified at: https://arxiv.org/abs/1005.4997 ; https://katalog.lib.cas.cz/KNAV/EdsRecord/edsarx,edsarx.1005.4997
- Note: Reports finding recursive structure in the segmentation of inscriptions using complex-network analysis. An earlier conference version is TextGraphs-4 (2009), pp. 5–13 (https://aclanthology.org/W09-3202/).

**D5.** Ashraf, Md Izhar & Sitabhra Sinha. 2018. "The 'handedness' of language: Directional symmetry breaking of sign usage in words." *PLoS ONE* 13(1): e0190735. DOI: 10.1371/journal.pone.0190735. arXiv: 1509.09121.
- Verified at: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5771592
- Note: Confirms across several writing systems a directional asymmetry in which the distribution of word-final signs is more constrained than that of word-initial signs. The measure can be applied to Indus problems such as determining the direction of writing.

**D6.** Palaniappan, Satish & Ronojoy Adhikari. 2017. "Deep learning the Indus script." arXiv: 1702.00523.
- Verified at: https://arxiv.org/abs/1702.00523v1
- Note: A CNN pipeline that locates text regions in photographs of artefacts and segments and classifies the signs. Labels were assigned using the Mahadevan corpus, and the code has been released (https://github.com/ronojoy/indus).

**D7.** Snyder, Benjamin, Regina Barzilay & Kevin Knight. 2010. "A statistical model for lost language decipherment." In *Proceedings of the 48th Annual Meeting of the Association for Computational Linguistics*, 1048–1057. Uppsala.
- Verified at: https://aclanthology.org/P10-1107/
- Note: A Bayesian model that jointly infers alphabetic correspondences and cognates using a non-parallel corpus of a related language. It was tested by matching Ugaritic with Hebrew.

**D8.** Luo, Jiaming, Yuan Cao & Regina Barzilay. 2019. "Neural decipherment via minimum-cost flow: From Ugaritic to Linear B." In *Proceedings of the 57th Annual Meeting of the Association for Computational Linguistics*, 3146–3155. Florence. DOI: 10.18653/v1/P19-1303.
- Verified at: https://aclanthology.org/P19-1303/ ; https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.06718
- Note: Formulates unsupervised learning as a minimum-cost flow problem and applies it to Ugaritic and Linear B.

**D9.** Luo, Jiaming, Frederik Hartmann, Enrico Santus, Regina Barzilay & Yuan Cao. 2021. "Deciphering undersegmented ancient scripts using phonetic prior." *Transactions of the Association for Computational Linguistics* 9: 69–81. DOI: 10.1162/tacl_a_00354.
- Verified at: https://aclanthology.org/2021.tacl-1.5 ; https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.11054v1
- Note: Deals with scripts that have no word segmentation and no known close relative, and applies the method to Iberian. The conditions are similar to those of the Indus script. (Knight is not among the authors; Santus is.)

**D10.** Daggumati, Shruti & Peter Z. Revesz. 2019. "Data mining ancient scripts to investigate their relationships and origins." In *Proceedings of the 23rd International Database Applications & Engineering Symposium (IDEAS '19)*. Athens.
- Verified at: https://researchr.org/publication/DaggumatiR19 ; https://cse.unl.edu/~revesz/papers/IDEAS19-a26.pdf
- Note: Compares several ancient scripts, including the Indus script, by their visual features.

**D11.** Daggumati, Shruti & Peter Z. Revesz. 2021. "A method of identifying allographs in undeciphered scripts and its application to the Indus Valley Script." *Humanities and Social Sciences Communications* 8(1): 1–11. DOI: 10.1057/s41599-021-00713-0.
- Verified at: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41599-021-00713-0 ; https://doaj.org/article/ca40175ebcba4bd4a983e485486b74ac
- Note: A method for finding allographs and reducing the sign list by mining positional information within inscriptions. It bears directly on the normalization of the sign list. (Volume and pages follow the RePEc record.)

**D12.** Daggumati, Shruti & Peter Z. Revesz. 2023. "Convolutional neural networks analysis reveals three possible sources of Bronze Age writings between Greece and India." *Information* 14: 227. DOI: 10.3390/info14040227.
- Verified at: https://www.mdpi.com/2078-2489/14/4/227 ; https://api.crossref.org/works/10.3390%2FINFO14040227
- Note: A follow-up study comparing the visual similarity of Bronze Age scripts with CNNs.

**D13.** Kumar, Vishal. 2024. "Review of computational epigraphy." arXiv: 2406.06570.
- Verified at: https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06570v1
- Note: A general overview of computational epigraphy, useful when choosing methods for Indus research.

**D14.** Reddy, Ooha Lakkadi. 2025. "Rerouting connection: Hybrid computer vision analysis reveals visual similarity between Indus and Tibetan-Yi Corridor writing systems." arXiv: 2503.21074.
- Verified at: https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.21074
- Note: A preprint that reports visual similarity between Indus signs and the scripts of the Tibetan-Yi Corridor using CNN-Transformer embeddings. The figures differ between versions, and visual similarity alone does not demonstrate a historical connection.

**D15.** Tiwari, Tanishk. 2026. "Statistical structure in Indus sign sequences." In *Proceedings of the 6th International Conference on Natural Language Processing for the Digital Humanities (NLP4DH 2026)*, 314–319. San Diego.
- Verified at: https://aclanthology.org/2026.nlp4dh-1.28/
- Note: Combines unsupervised visual clustering of sign shapes with sequence statistics on a corpus of 6,579 inscriptions.

**D16.** Nair, Ashish. 2026. "How non-linguistic is the Indus sign system? A synthetic-baseline scorecard." arXiv: 2604.17828.
- Verified at: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.17828 ; https://arxiv.org/html/2604.17828
- Note: Compares an Indus corpus (1,916 deduplicated inscriptions, 584 signs, 11,110 tokens) with synthetic baselines of the heraldic and administrative-code types and with real non-linguistic corpora (including the Sproat 2014 data). It reports that the Indus corpus occupies an intermediate position that does not fit cleanly with any non-linguistic model. The data were extracted from ICIT via the Yajnadevam digitization. It is a preprint that has not yet been peer-reviewed.

**D17.** Raghavendra, Nikhil. 2026. "On the non-specificity of statistical measures used in script decipherment." arXiv: 2608.02999.
- Verified at: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.02999
- Note: Reports that SIGIL, a generated sign system with meaning but no sound values (3,000 texts), matches the Indus corpus on tests of repetition, directional asymmetry and vocabulary distribution, and argues that such measures detect organization but are not specific to language. A preprint of which only the abstract was checked.
