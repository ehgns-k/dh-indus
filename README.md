# dh-indus: research repository for the decipherment of the Indus script

This repository records an attempt to analyse the Indus script material with reproducible methods and to attempt a decipherment according to staged success criteria. The research plan and success criteria are in [ROADMAP.md](ROADMAP.md), the working method in [docs/METHOD.md](docs/METHOD.md), and the dated research log in [docs/JOURNAL.md](docs/JOURNAL.md).

## Current status

The Indus script is undeciphered. Results are labelled with the levels of [ROADMAP.md](ROADMAP.md) (L1 structure, L2 semantic or functional categories, L3 sound values, L4 full decipherment). The summary below is kept up to date; every test, including the failed ones, is in [docs/JOURNAL.md](docs/JOURNAL.md), and the living final report is [reports/FINAL_REPORT.md](reports/FINAL_REPORT.md).

| Level | Status | Main evidence |
|---|---|---|
| L1 structure | **achieved** | Right-to-left order predicts later finds (F3), holds across the two main sites (SX1) and also for lines written left to right once normalised (DIR1); initial–medial–closing template (B1, Q2a), which with the recurring units carries over from seals and tablets to tags, bangles and other objects in both transcriptions (PT2, PT2k) and, for the units, to pottery (PT1); closing cluster with fixed order in every object class (H3.3, H3.4, H3.3c); template and units hold when each main site is left out (SX3, SX4); stroke numerals with count-specific partner signs (H3.10–H3.12, F2); formulae with a variable number slot (H9.1, R4); substitution classes reproduced across transcriptions (H9.3); nested structure of longer texts (CT18); near-variant shapes used alike (G2); the fish-shaped signs form one usage class in both transcriptions (CT22, CT22k); the two lines of a two-line inscription, and the two faces of a two-face object, form one text, closed once (ML1, ML1k, FACE1, FACE4); Harappa tablets with the same text tend to carry the same count on their numeral face, though the count varies within a series (FACE2); the formulae recur in both transcriptions (KF1) and, learned from either transcription, in later finds (F5, F5k). The core results survive a Holm correction at 0.01 over all 47 tests on unused data (R7) |
| L2 meaning / function | **narrow, functional only** | The jar sign 740 closes seal legends more often than tablet texts at Harappa and Mohenjo-daro (site-stratified; the 'other sites' stratum has a single tablet text) (CT15, p = 0.0022; survives Holm at 0.05 and false-discovery control at 0.01 over 47 tests, not Holm at 0.01; R7), and tags with seal-type legends follow the seal pattern (CT15t); seal legends are individual-specific, tablet texts repeat (CT28). What the closing sign means is not established. Other candidates: closing sign by object class (C2, C2k), a sign class absent from copper tablets (H9.4). Object size, a new external variable, is predicted by no sign on unused data (SZ2) |
| L3 sound values | **not achieved** | No calibrated phonetic method could identify even a known language hidden as a cipher (E3–E3d); a published Sanskrit reading is no better than random assignments (E2, E2b) |
| Writing or not | **undecided** | The closest reference system is proto-Elamite in both transcriptions (D1, D1-KP), whose estimated sign inventory is also comparable (D3); a formula-based comparison did not meet its criterion (D5, 13/20); frequent signs combine less freely than syllabic graphemes, as logograms or a non-linguistic system would (CT27), although where this places the Indus texts among the reference systems depends on the transcription (D7); the numeral precedes the sign it goes with, the reverse of the proto-Elamite order (NUM1); the statistics cannot separate writing from non-linguistic systems, also with a seventh statistic (D2, D2b, CT19) |

Published claims: verdicts on all 35 claims of the claims register are in [docs/CLAIMS_EVALUATION.md](docs/CLAIMS_EVALUATION.md).

## Layout

```
ROADMAP.md              research phases, success criteria
docs/METHOD.md          work units, cycle, rules
docs/JOURNAL.md         dated research log
docs/BIBLIOGRAPHY.md    verified bibliography
docs/SIGN_CONCORDANCE.md  sign-number concordance between the two corpora
docs/SIGN_CLASSES.md    frequent signs with their established roles
docs/FORMULAE.md        formulae with a variable slot and their fillers (ICIT)
docs/FORMULAE_KP.md     the same for KP1979
docs/COMBINED_INVENTORY.md  sign list combining the allograph decisions of both transcriptions
docs/CLAIMS_EVALUATION.md  verdicts on the 35 published claims of the claims register
data/sources.tsv        list of raw sources (pinned commit, SHA-256)
scripts/fetch_data.py   download and verify the raw sources
src/indus/corpus.py     normalise both corpora into lines in reading order
scripts/phase*_*.py     analyses by phase
results/                analysis output (generated by the scripts)
reports/                phase reports and review reports
```

See `docs/REPRODUCIBILITY.md`. `scripts/run_all.py` re-runs every analysis and lists result files that differ from the committed versions.

## Data sources

The raw data are not included in this repository. `scripts/fetch_data.py` downloads the pinned versions listed in `data/sources.tsv` and checks their checksums.

- ICIT-style secondary transcription: <https://github.com/horus84/Computational_Linguistics> (commit `ac44f08`)
- Machine reading of Koskenniemi & Parpola (1979), *Corpus of Texts in the Indus Script*: <https://github.com/koskenni/indus> (commit `60f6e28`)

Reference corpora for the typology phase come from the CDLI bulk data (<https://github.com/cdli-gh/data>, August 2022), fetched by `scripts/fetch_cdli.py`. Non-linguistic reference corpora come from Sproat (2014) (<https://github.com/rwsproat/non-linguistic-symbols>, Apache License 2.0), fetched by `scripts/fetch_sproat.py`.

The two sources use different sign-numbering systems. Neither replaces the original photographs or a primary concordance.
