### 1.2 The L2 candidates

1. **The closing sign depends on the kind of object** (C2, C2k): 400 is avoided on motif-bearing seals and favoured on tablets; 407 and 154 close texts on rectangular seals without motif and on copper tablets. Consistent in two transcriptions and two object classifications, with the same effect sizes after near-duplicate removal; not confirmed at the strict thresholds.
2. **A substitution class containing 002 and 090 is absent from copper tablets** (H9.4), confirmed on the strict held-out split, but the class has no structural interpretation fixed in advance.

### 1.3 What the numerals show (structural, with a semantic reading)

Stroke signs behave like a numeral notation in several independent respects: counts read from the glyphs agree with both numbering systems; specific counts go with specific following signs (H3.10, H3.12, F2); texts identical except for the count are six to eight times more frequent than chance, often differing by two or more strokes (H9.1); the numerals form one substitution class (H9.3). Formulae with a variable count occur on seals as much as on tablets (H9.2), so the numerals are not confined to record-like objects.

Not met. Two candidates exist; confirmation would need unused inscriptions of seals and tablets, which are not available in accessible form (`docs/NEW_DATA_SURVEY.md`).

## 2. Sound: phonetic decipherment (L3)

| Test | Finding |
|---|---|
| E2 | Under the normalisation of a published Sanskrit reading (claim C33), 29 % of random letter strings of the same composition divide into Sanskrit dictionary words (proposed readings: 84 %). |
| E2b | Random, unfitted sign-value assignments with the same reading freedoms make 84 % of Indus lines readable, as many as the published readings. |
| E3, E3b, E3c, E3d | A calibrated search for sign-to-sound assignments under Sanskrit, Old Tamil, Sumerian and Akkadian sound models cannot identify the language of known cuneiform texts hidden as ciphers (2,000 and 8,000 lines; unequal and equalised models). No language preference is claimed for the Indus inscriptions. |

Gate G4: not met. Under the standards of this project no calibrated route to a phonetic decipherment is available with the accessible data. Any future attempt needs an independent constraint (bilingual evidence, externally attested names, or pictographic values fixed in advance) and must show, with known scripts hidden as ciphers, that the method can identify the right language.
