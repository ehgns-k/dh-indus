# Interim report 6 (2026-11-25, night)

This report summarises the twenty-seventh to thirtieth working sessions. Details are in `docs/JOURNAL.md`.

## 1. Structure (L1)

| Test | Result |
|---|---|
| DIR1, DIR2 | Lines written left to right (105 of 122) and lines of unknown direction (84 of 127) follow the normalised reading order. The direction of writing varies; the order of the text does not. |
| SX1 | Order regularities learned at the other sites predict Harappa and Mohenjo-daro forward far better than reversed. |
| PT1–PT3, PT2k, PT3k | The template, the recurring units and the count–item pairing learned from seals and tablets carry over to tags, bangles, rods and other objects, in both transcriptions. Pottery shares the units and the pairing; the short pottery texts give too little power for the template. |

## 2. Function (L2)

## 3. Typology

| Test | Result |
|---|---|
| D2b | A seventh statistic (combinatorial density) does not make the D2 method separate writing from non-writing. |
| D6, D6b | The specificity of numeral partners cannot be compared fairly with the proto-administrative systems, because the measure depends on the size of the numeral vocabulary. |
| D8, D9, D10 | In order rigidity, positional rigidity and rank–frequency shape, the Indus corpus lies inside the range of the reference corpora, not at an extreme. |

## 4. Position

The level assessment is unchanged: L1 achieved, L2 only in the narrow functional form of CT15, and L3 not achieved. The statistical typology repeatedly places the Indus corpus among the proto-administrative and full-writing corpora, but no measure tried separates writing from non-linguistic systems. Whether the signs record a language remains open.
